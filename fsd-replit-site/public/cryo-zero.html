What is whole-body cryotherapy?

Whole-body cryotherapy involves brief exposure to very cold, dry air in a purpose-built chamber. It is not a bath or an ice pack. Experiences vary, and research on possible recovery benefits is limited and mixed.

This page does not present cryotherapy as a medical treatment or claim that it treats, cures or prevents illness or injury. It is not a substitute for medical advice.

Equipment details to follow. Chamber design, temperatures and operating requirements will be published once confirmed against the manufacturer’s documentation.

How a visit will work

A Cryo-Zero visit is planned around four stages. Full procedures will follow approved safety guidance for the chamber in use.

01

Screening

A short health and eligibility check before every first session.

02

Preparation

Protective clothing and clear preparation instructions before you enter the chamber.

03

Supervised session

Every session is supervised by a trained operator, and you can stop at any time.

04

Aftercare

Simple guidance after your session on warming up and returning to training.

Safety and evidence

Safety information

Extreme cold can carry risks, including cold injury, light-headedness, skin sensations, anxiety or discomfort. Final risk information, screening criteria and contraindications require review against supplier guidance and insurer requirements before any service is offered.

Evidence and health claims

Research on the recovery benefits of cryotherapy is limited and mixed. FSD makes no medical claims for Cryo-Zero.
